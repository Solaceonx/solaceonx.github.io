Tomida et al. (2026)

arXiv:2605.07928 ↗

Constrained transport against mixed divergence cleaning. Kelvin–Helmholtz instability with a weak field: uniform, confined to one layer, and with a sudden timestep change. The paper's CT, D1v/Dhv (cleaning speed following the timestep) and D1c (constant speed) correspond to our CT prototype and AthenaK/RAMSES/Athena++, the four GLM kernels with c_h ∝ 1/Δt, and the constant-c_h kernel.

Sudden timestep change: the cleaning variable against time, and the field it leaves behind, N = 256

Same test

Left: largest |ψ| at every output and, from the timestep change (dashed line) to 300 steps after it, at every step. Middle: the same around the change. Right: median |B| inside the field-free upper vortex just before the change (open marks) and at the end of the run (filled), with their ratio; grey line: the reference at t = 3.

In these runs: With a cleaning speed that follows 1/Δt the largest ψ jumps 15- to 59-fold, and at the end of the run the field inside the field-free vortex is 9.19 to 223 times what it was before the change on 512² (1.95 to 45 times on 256², 1.1 to 27 on 128²). With the constant speed ψ changes by a factor 1.2 to 1.5 and that field by 0.8 to 1.1; in the CT prototype the field changes by 1.1 to 1.2. The paper reports about a 40-fold jump of ψ in its variable-speed models and no effect with a constant speed or CT.

Mach 9 decay: spectra of the 256³ runs divided by the 512³ reference

Same measurement, different set-up

Velocity (left) and magnetic (right) power spectra of each 256³ run divided by the 512³ reference's at the same time, at the first output (t = 0.02, the paper's time) and at the end. Dotted lines: the paper's ±25 % tolerance; the wavenumber where a curve leaves it, as a fraction of the Nyquist wavenumber, is the effective bandwidth (table below). At t = 0.02 our smooth start has only just formed its first shocks, so t = 0.2 is the fairer time to read.

The paper's reference is its least diffusive code at 1024³ filtered to 256³; ours is AthenaK PLM/HLLE at 512³, unfiltered, which is itself a second-order scheme.
